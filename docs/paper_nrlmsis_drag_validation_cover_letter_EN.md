We submit the manuscript "Validating NRLMSIS 2.1 Orbital-Decay Predictions With 425 Passive LEO Satellites and FORMOSAT-7 Precise Orbits: Activity-Dependent Bias and Implications for Drag-Residual Screening Thresholds" for consideration as a Research Article in *Space Weather*.

Drag-residual screening — subtracting model-predicted decay from TLE-observed decay to detect satellite maneuvers — is increasingly used for space situational awareness, yet the density model it relies on is seldom tested under the conditions of that use. Over January 2024 to November 2026, a period spanning the Solar Cycle 25 maximum, we test NRLMSIS 2.1 against 425 perigee-binned satellites at 300–1000 km and FORMOSAT-7 precise orbits. The main results are:

1. With one ballistic coefficient per satellite, NRLMSIS 2.1 reproduces the solar-activity variation of passive-satellite decay (median per-satellite correlation 0.92), confirmed independently of TLEs by precise orbits (r = 0.962).
2. The ratio of predicted to observed decay rises with solar flux by about 17% between low- and high-flux periods, consistently across 89% of 265 passive satellites and in FORMOSAT-7 precise orbits.
3. A fixed drag-residual threshold over-alarms below 450 km and is insensitive above 600 km; altitude-tuned thresholds evaluated out of sample do not fully generalize, and contamination of the passive reference population by small station-keeping is identified as a primary limitation.

These results are directly relevant to operators and analysts who use thermospheric models for orbit prediction and maneuver screening during periods of high solar activity.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. Two companion manuscripts, on TLE noise floors (submitted to *Acta Astronautica*) and on the detection sensitivity of TLE-based maneuver detection (submitted to *Advances in Space Research*), address different questions and do not overlap in data analysis.
