We submit the manuscript "Validating NRLMSIS 2.1 Orbital-Decay Predictions With 425 Perigee-Binned LEO Satellites and FORMOSAT-7 Precise Orbits: Activity-Dependent Bias and Implications for Drag-Residual Screening Thresholds" for consideration as a Research Article in *Space Weather*.

Drag-residual screening, which subtracts model-predicted from TLE-observed decay to detect maneuvers, needs a density model evaluated across the solar-activity range in which it operates. From 1 January 2024 through 14 November 2026, spanning the Solar Cycle 25 maximum, we test NRLMSIS 2.1 against 425 perigee-binned satellites at 300–1000 km, 309 of which have no adjacent-TLE semi-major-axis increase exceeding 0.3 km and are classified as nominally passive, and against FORMOSAT-7 precise orbits. With one effective ballistic coefficient fitted per satellite over the full period, we evaluate within-satellite Pearson correlations and high-to-low flux ratios of predicted-to-observed decay. These metrics are invariant to the fitted multiplicative scale for a fixed set of windows; absolute density accuracy is not assessed. The main results are:

1. NRLMSIS 2.1 tracks the temporal variation of observed decay (median per-satellite correlation 0.92; 309 nominally passive satellites). FORMOSAT-7 precise orbits, independent of the TLEs, corroborate this (per-satellite correlations 0.958–0.967; 0.962 pooled over five satellites and 616 windows).
2. The median per-satellite high-to-low flux ratio of predicted-to-observed decay is 1.17 (95% CI 1.16–1.19), comparing 81-day mean F10.7 above 160 sfu with below 120 sfu. The direction of change is the same in 235 (89%) of the 265 nominally passive satellites with sufficient data in both groups and is corroborated by FORMOSAT-7 precise orbits. The association remains partly confounded with time and altitude.
3. A fixed 0.3 km residual threshold flags nominally passive satellites much more frequently below 450 km, whereas above 600 km its 90% residual-level injection detection floor of approximately 0.31 km greatly exceeds the metre-level residuals. These flag rates are not validated false-positive rates. In satellite-grouped and temporal out-of-sample tests, no altitude-dependent scheme meets the benchmark flagging rate in every evaluable altitude bin. Flags concentrate on a few propulsion-equipped satellites, suggesting possible contamination of the nominally passive reference population by small station-keeping maneuvers.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. Two related manuscripts have been submitted: one on TLE semi-major-axis noise floors (*Acta Astronautica*) and one on the detection sensitivity of TLE-based maneuver detection calibrated against FORMOSAT-7 precise orbits (*Advances in Space Research*). They share data sources with the present work (Space-Track TLEs, and FORMOSAT-7 leoOrb precise orbits in the second), but their primary analyses address distinct questions: neither evaluates NRLMSIS decay predictions, their dependence on solar activity, or drag-residual thresholds.
