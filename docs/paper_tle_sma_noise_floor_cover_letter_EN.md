We submit the manuscript "Empirical Characterization of TLE-Derived Semi-Major-Axis Noise from DORIS-Certified Quiescent Arcs and Its Application to FORMOSAT Maneuver Analysis" for consideration as a research article in *Acta Astronautica*.

Maneuver screening from public Two-Line Elements (TLEs) thresholds the change in the TLE-derived semi-major axis, yet the noise level behind such thresholds is rarely measured on intervals known to be free of maneuvers. This manuscript measures it on operator-certified maneuver-free arcs of thirteen International DORIS Service altimetry satellites and reports:

1. A median noise floor of 0.24 m at 719–1338 km, two orders of magnitude below fixed thresholds of tens of metres in operational use, robust under 27 processing configurations.
2. An altitude dependence with a 77 km scale height, within the range of thermospheric density scale heights, and a 0.9 m threshold that detects 67 of 68 certified Jason-3 maneuver windows.
3. A test against IDS three-component Δv truth showing that along-track Δv is recoverable from TLEs on clean arcs (r = 0.942) whereas cross-track Δv is not, and an application to the FORMOSAT series that separates genuine maneuvers from constellation-wide steps.

The results provide a measured basis for altitude-dependent, σ-normalized detection thresholds in space situational awareness.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. A companion manuscript on the detection sensitivity of TLE-based maneuver detection, calibrated against FORMOSAT-7 precise orbits, has been submitted to *Advances in Space Research* (reference 14); the two papers do not overlap in data analysis.
